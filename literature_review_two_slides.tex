
\begin{frame}{Related Work: What the Field Already Provides}
\small
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Generalist VLA policies} such as RT-2, OpenVLA, Octo, and pi\_0 can map images and language to robot actions, but their scene and physics reasoning is mostly implicit.
\item \textbf{Tool-augmented robot reasoning} such as SayCan, Code as Policies, VoxPoser, and MOKA shows that external tools can ground model decisions in affordances, geometry, or control APIs.
\item \textbf{3D scene graph and GraphRAG methods} such as SayPlan, RoboEXP, KeySG, Structured Interfaces, FunGraph, and ArtiSG show that structured scene memory supports grounding, retrieval, functional reasoning, and action-conditioned exploration.
\item \textbf{Safety and contact-rich manipulation work} such as SAFE, FailSafe, SafeManip, ForceVLA, and physics-driven data generation shows that success rate alone is insufficient; contact, force, stability, and temporal safety matter.
\item \textbf{Simulation benchmarks} such as Open X-Embodiment, RoboCasa, RoboCAS, and RoboTwin provide scale and task diversity, but usually do not export graph-grounded physics, risk, belief, and tool-use supervision.
\end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Proposal-Relevant Gaps}
\small
\textbf{Core gap.} Existing systems provide strong pieces, but they rarely combine persistent 4D scene memory, belief-aware retrieval, physics verification, and VLA tool calling in one framework.

\vspace{0.6em}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Hallucination gap}: VLA decisions are often produced from model predictions alone; they are not consistently grounded by external physics and scene verification tools.
\item \textbf{Scene-physics gap}: existing policies rarely expose explicit support, containment, occlusion, contact, and object-relationship structure to the action generator.
\item \textbf{Safety-verification gap}: collision checking, stability analysis, and unsafe-contact detection are often separate from the learned policy or applied only after failure.
\item \textbf{Belief gap}: partial observability and hidden scene states are usually not represented as explicit belief nodes that can guide retrieval and action revision.
\item \textbf{Long-horizon memory gap}: many systems lack action history, state transitions, relation deltas, and causal effects needed for robust multi-step manipulation.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.4em}
\textbf{Positioning.} Our proposal addresses these gaps with a 4D action-aware Task-driven Belief Graph, local graph retrieval, and physics-based tool calls before execution.
\end{frame}
